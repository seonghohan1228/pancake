## Metric definitions

- `W = sqrt(Fpx^2 + Fpy^2)` and the force angle `atan2(Fpy, Fpx)` are read from the
  `Fp` vector array of the final flat VTK (the solver fills these as uniform fields;
  they are not columns of `diagnostics.csv`, contrary to the README wording).
- `p_max` is the largest cell-centre value of `p` in the final flat VTK.
- Cavitation area fraction counts cells with `film_content < 0.999`; the mesh is
  uniform, so the cell-count fraction is the area fraction.
- THD temperatures are cell-centre extrema and the arithmetic cell mean of `T`.
- 00b eccentricity ratio is `sqrt(xB^2 + yB^2) / c` from the `xB` array; settling
  time is the first VTK output time after which `|e(t) - e_final| < 0.01` holds for
  the remainder of the record.

Notes on the THD pair:

1. **The committed `config.txt` in both THD directories fails validation and cannot
   run.** With `fluid_property_model = SINGLE_PHASE` the solver requires
   `dissolved_gas_henry_coeff > 0` and a non-zero `solution_viscosity_gas_coeff`,
   but both are 0 in the committed files, so the run aborts with
   "Configuration failed validation". The originals were left untouched; a
   `config_thd_fixed.txt` sits beside each, identical except for those two keys set
   to `1e-12`. Both are inert here (`dissolved_gas_initial = 0`, gas transport is a
   no-op outside `TWO_PHASE`, and the viscosity term is `exp(a_c * c_g)` with
   `c_g = 0`), so the physics is unchanged. **This needs a real fix in the case
   configs or in the validator before the campaign proceeds.**
2. **These runs are not exercising thermo-viscous coupling.** The energy equation is
   active and produces a large temperature rise (313.15 K initial -> 374-379 K), but
   W changes by only 0.006% and the friction torque by 0.004% against the isothermal
   runs on the same grid. The reason is the viscosity law: the solver implements
   Andrade, `mu = mu_ref * exp[E_mu (1/T - 1/T_ref)]`, so
   `viscosity_temperature_coeff` has units of K; the configs set it to 0.034. For a
   65 K rise that gives `exp(0.034 * -5.6e-4) = 1 - 1.9e-5`, i.e. no effect. A value
   of 0.034 is the standard *linear* coefficient `beta` in 1/K for
   `mu = mu_0 exp(-beta dT)`; the Andrade equivalent for an ISO VG 32 oil is of
   order 3000-4500 K. As configured, the THD runs are energy-equation-only and give
   no independent grid check of the coupled problem. Worth resolving before the
   Ferron comparison.
3. Grid behaviour of the THD pair mirrors the isothermal pair exactly (p_max falls
   1.54% from G3_thd to G4_thd), and the temperature field itself is essentially
   grid independent: T_max changes by 0.053 K (0.014%) and T_mean by 0.050 K
   (0.013%) between the two grids.

### The bearing settles onto a limit cycle, not a fixed point

This is the load-bearing observation for the rest of the study.

By the stated criterion T1 settles at **t = 0.01952 s**, comfortably inside both the
0.05 s gate and the 0.06 s window. But it settles onto a **persistent oscillation**,
not a steady state. After t = 0.02 s the eccentricity holds
e = 0.4816 +- 0.0013 with a peak-to-peak of 0.0123 - so the cycle amplitude, 1.2% of
the clearance, sits essentially *on* the 1% settling tolerance. The criterion is
passed marginally, by a metric that is measuring the cycle rather than a fixed point.

The pressure force swings far harder than the position does, because the 10 kg
bearing mass filters it: W = 6219 +- 3584 N, ranging 1631 to 11536 N about the
6000 N load.

Two consequences:

1. **Single-frame metrics are not reproducible.** `e_final` and `W_final` are one
   arbitrary sample of the cycle. `e_final` reads 0.48264 at 0.06 s and 0.48280 at
   0.15 s; `W_final` reads 10724 N and 9397 N at those same two times. Comparing
   timesteps on final-frame values would compare sampling phase, not physics.
   `analyse_dt_fbns.py` therefore computes the dt-to-dt relative change from the
   post-settling **mean** (`w060.e_mean`), and reports `e_std` / `e_peak_to_peak`
   alongside it.
2. **Force balance is sound in the mean.** +3.65% over the full record. The
   +7.35% figure over the 0-0.06 s window is the same quantity averaged over fewer
   cycles, not a different result.

### The oscillation is numerical, and it is what the dt ladder now measures

A DFT of the per-step `cavitated_fraction` over t >= 0.05 s puts the dominant
content at **~12.43 kHz**, against a step-rate Nyquist of 1/(2 dt) = 12.5 kHz, with
a secondary band near 1.97 kHz. Content at essentially 2 dt is the signature of a
step-to-step numerical oscillation. It is not physical whirl: the shaft turns at
66.7 Hz, so half-frequency whirl would appear near 33 Hz, two and a half decades
below what is there.

The prediction this sets up for T2-T4 is direct - if the oscillation is numerical,
its frequency should track 1/(2 dt) as dt falls, and its amplitude in e should
shrink. If instead the amplitude is dt-independent, the cycle is coming from the
cavitation-front treatment or the motion coupling rather than from temporal
resolution, and the ladder will not converge. Either way the mean eccentricity is
the quantity to compare.

Note the contrast with the superseded G3/JFO runs above, which reached a genuinely
steady force balance (6024.9 N, 0.4% high, stable). The oscillation is new with
FBNS on G6; whether it comes from the model or the grid is not separable from these
two runs alone.

### Verdict: the oscillation was temporal resolution, and it is gone by T2

T2-T4 settle the question. Measured on the per-step `cavitated_fraction` over the
common 0.03-0.06 s window, **linearly detrended first** (see the caveat below), the
2 dt content collapses on the first halving of dt and never comes back:

| run | dt [s] | amplitude at the Nyquist bin | step-to-step alternation | raw cav_frac p2p | e peak-to-peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dt_T1_fbns | 4e-5 | 5.47e-3 | 1.77e-2 | 1.516e-1 | 0.00466 |
| dt_T2_fbns | 2e-5 | 2.42e-8 | 3.53e-7 | 4.196e-4 | 0.00247 |
| dt_T3_fbns | 1e-5 | 4.72e-8 | 1.96e-7 | 4.196e-4 | 0.00248 |
| dt_T4_fbns | 5e-6 | 4.74e-9 | 1.02e-7 | 4.340e-4 | 0.00248 |

Five orders of magnitude between T1 and T2. **The limit cycle was a 2 dt
artifact of temporal resolution, not the FBNS cavitation front and not the motion
coupling.** No defect report is warranted against the cavitation model on this
evidence.

At dt <= 2e-5 the bearing reaches a genuine fixed point, not a cycle: over
0.03-0.06 s the eccentricity is a monotone settling drift (e = 0.48080 -> 0.48302 in
T2), and the residual `e` peak-to-peak of 0.0025 is that drift, not an oscillation.
The T1-era instruction to compare post-settling means rather than final frames
remains correct practice, but from T2 onward the single-frame values are reproducible
anyway.

**Trap worth recording.** A naive `argmax` on the spectrum reports a 33.3 Hz peak for
T2, T3 and T4 alike, which looks exactly like half-frequency whirl - the shaft turns
at 66.7 Hz. It is not. Over a 0.03 s window the first non-zero DFT bin *is* 33.3 Hz,
so that "peak" is residual settling drift landing in bin 1. The window has to be
detrended before any whirl claim is made; after detrending the strongest sub-Nyquist
content sits at 100 Hz with amplitude ~1.6e-5, which is negligible.

The `cav_frac` raw peak-to-peak reads exactly 4.195602e-4 for both T2 and T3. That is
quantisation, not a stuck field: `cavitated_fraction` moves in units of one cell,
1/138240 = 7.2338e-6, and 4.195602e-4 is exactly 58 cells.

### Sampling caveat - applies to T1 only, resolved for T2-T4

`write_interval = 5e-4` s is 12.5 steps at dt = 4e-5 - not an integer - so T1's VTK
frames sample the limit cycle at a drifting phase and alias it. That aliasing is why
adjacent frames in the T1 record jump between W ~ 1.6 kN and W ~ 11.5 kN while e
barely moves. `diagnostics.csv` is written every step but carries **no bearing
position**, so the cycle cannot be characterised per-step from the CSV alone.

This does **not** affect T2-T4: 5e-4 s is exactly 25, 50 and 100 steps at
dt = 2e-5, 1e-5 and 5e-6, so every remote run samples at a fixed phase. It is also
moot now that the cycle is known to be a T1 artifact.

Still worth doing for future transient work:

- log `xB` (and ideally the pressure resultant) in `diagnostics.csv`, so bearing
  motion can be characterised per-step instead of through VTK frames, and
- keep `write_interval` an integer multiple of `dt`.

### On `outer_iters` under FBNS

The `outer_iters` column of `diagnostics.csv` reports **Newton iterations capped by
`fbns_max_iters` (default 100)**, not the Elrod-Adams `max_outer_iters` (set to 50
in these configs). Confirmed in source: `src/fbns.cpp:382` loops to
`cfg.fbns_max_iters`, while `max_outer_iters` is consumed only by the Elrod-Adams
path at `src/reynolds.cpp:422`. The value of 61 seen in T0 and 77 in T1 are
therefore legitimate counts well inside the 100 cap, **not** cap violations.

### Cost note

Cost per step **falls** as dt falls, because Newton iterations per step fall with it
(mean 20.18 -> 2.03 -> 1.81 -> 1.62 across T1-T4). Halving dt therefore costs
appreciably less than 2x: on 24 pinned ranks the three remote runs sustained 4.25,
4.26 and 4.74 steps/s, so T4 did 4x T2's steps in 3.6x T2's wall clock. The whole
T2+T3+T4 sweep took 4648 s (77.5 min), against 31070 s (8 h 37 m) for T1 alone on 4
local ranks.

Two estimation traps were hit and are worth recording:

1. **Do not estimate from the opening steps.** An early estimate from T1's first 32
   steps read 3.5 s/step and understated the true cost by 2.4x, because Newton
   iterations climb through T1's transient (by quarter: 5.6, 15.1, 24.2, 24.8).
2. **Do not estimate from a short window at all without separating startup.** A
   10-step probe of T2 is ~5.6 s of fixed MPI/PETSc startup on top of the real work,
   which at n=24 is 0.235 s/step - so the probe overstated per-step cost by ~3.5x and
   would have ranked the rank counts on startup cost rather than solve cost.

### Threading: a silent order-of-magnitude performance trap on Linux

Found while calibrating the remote runs, and it invalidates any naive scaling study
on this build. The Ubuntu PETSc 3.15.5 links **OpenBLAS and libgomp**. With no thread
limit set, **every MPI rank spawns ~30 threads**: 8 ranks drove the node to a load
average of 205 with each rank at ~690% CPU.

The damage is strongly rank-dependent, because OpenBLAS only multithreads above a
problem-size threshold - so it fires at low rank counts, where each rank owns a large
theta strip, and stays dormant at high rank counts. Measured on 10 steps of the T2
configuration:

| ranks | unpinned | `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` | penalty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 376.66 s | 31.93 s | **11.8x** |
| 24 | 8.27 s | 8.00 s | 1.035x |

Unpinned, this manufactures *apparent superlinear speedup* - n=4 to n=24 looked like
a 44x gain for a 6x rank increase. Pinned, the same measurement is an ordinary,
sublinear 4.4x.

**Always launch with
`mpirun -x OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 -x OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`** on this class of build.
Because the n=24 penalty is only 3.5%, the T2 production run (taken before the trap
was found) is not materially affected and was not repeated; T3 and T4 were run
pinned.

### Strong scaling on afml-r6 (2 x Xeon E5-2660 v4)

T2 configuration, 50-step window, threads pinned, runs executed one at a time.
Marginal cost is `(t50 - t10) / 40`, which removes fixed startup. Efficiency is
against n = 4.

| ranks | wall, 50 steps [s] | steps/s | marginal [s/step] | efficiency | theta decomposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 102.82 | 0.486 | 1.783 | 100% | 180 exact |
| 8 | 56.34 | 0.888 | 0.973 | 92% | 90 exact |
| 16 | 35.56 | 1.406 | 0.614 | 73% | 45 exact |
| **24** | **24.38** | **2.051** | **0.410** | **73%** | **30 exact** |
| 32 | 32.26 | 1.550 | 0.548 | 41% | 16x23 + 16x22, hyperthreaded |
| 48 | 24.46 | 2.044 | 0.416 | 36% | 15 exact, hyperthreaded |
| 56 | 26.62 | 1.878 | 0.449 | 28% | 48x13 + 8x12, hyperthreaded |

The knee is the **28 physical core** boundary. `nproc` reports 56, but that is
hyperthreads on 2 x 14 cores, so OpenMPI refuses n > 28 without
`--use-hwthread-cpus`; past that point two ranks share a physical core and efficiency
collapses below 41%. n = 32 is *slower in absolute wall clock* than n = 24, and n = 48
merely ties n = 24 at half the efficiency.

**n = 24 is the operating point**: fastest wall clock, 73% efficiency, an exact
720/24 = 30 cells per rank, and 4 cores left free. Decomposition is a block
distribution in which the first `remainder` ranks take one extra theta cell
(`src/mesh.cpp`), so n = 32 and n = 56 carry 4.5% and 8.3% load imbalance on top of
the hyperthreading penalty.

## Flags for the user

1. `p_max` is not grid independent on G1-G5: 1.1-1.6% change per level, apparent
   order 0.137, fine-grid GCI 1.77% even at an assumed p = 2. W, torque and
   cavitation fraction are converged. (README guidance is <1% for both W and p_max.)
2. Both THD `config.txt` files fail solver validation as committed and were run from
   a `config_thd_fixed.txt` variant; see the 00a notes.
3. `viscosity_temperature_coeff = 0.034` is being consumed as an Andrade coefficient
   in K, which makes it inert. The THD runs therefore do not test thermo-viscous
   coupling.
4. The 00b sweep was stopped and is superseded; on the G3 grid it had not
   demonstrated dt independence (change in equilibrium eccentricity grew from
   +0.099% to +0.713% with refinement).
5. `write_interval = 0.005` s is too coarse to resolve settling time for the dt
   study; consider 5e-4 s when the sweep is redone.
6. The equilibrium pressure resultant is consistently ~0.4% above the 6000 N applied
   load in every dt run.
7. **(00b-FBNS)** `dt = 8e-5` is above the stability ceiling on G6 with FBNS: the
   bearing overshoots the clearance sevenfold and the film guard stops the run at
   t = 0.0041 s. The ladder starts at 4e-5.
8. **(00b-FBNS)** T1 settles onto a limit cycle rather than a fixed point. The
   cycle amplitude in eccentricity (1.2% of c) sits on the 1% settling tolerance,
   and the pressure resultant swings 1.6-11.5 kN about the 6 kN load while the
   position barely moves. Single-frame `e_final` / `W_final` are not reproducible;
   compare timesteps on the post-settling mean. **Resolved by T2-T4: this is a T1
   artifact.** At dt <= 2e-5 the bearing reaches a fixed point and single-frame
   values are reproducible again.
9. **(00b-FBNS)** **Closed.** The T1 oscillation at ~12.43 kHz against a 12.5 kHz
   step-rate Nyquist was a 2 dt temporal-resolution artifact, not the FBNS
   cavitation front and not the motion coupling. Halving dt once drops the Nyquist
   -bin amplitude from 5.47e-3 to 2.42e-8 and it stays there through T4. No defect
   report against the cavitation model is warranted.
10. **(00b-FBNS)** `diagnostics.csv` carries no bearing position, so bearing motion
    can only be seen through VTK frames. Still worth fixing by logging `xB` per
    step. The companion `write_interval` aliasing concern applies to T1 only -
    5e-4 s is an exact 25 / 50 / 100 steps for T2 / T3 / T4.
11. **(00b-FBNS)** Runtime estimates are easy to get wrong in both directions. Cost
    per step *grows* through T1's transient as Newton iterations climb (5.6 ->
    24.8 mean by quarter), so estimates from the opening steps understate by ~2.4x;
    but a short probe is also dominated by ~5.6 s of fixed MPI/PETSc startup, which
    overstates per-step cost by ~3.5x. Measure a developed transient with startup
    subtracted.
12. **(00b-FBNS, cross-cutting)** **Threading trap on Linux.** PETSc here links
    OpenBLAS + libgomp and every rank spawns ~30 threads unless told otherwise;
    unpinned, n=4 runs 11.8x slower and the node hits load average 205. It also
    fakes superlinear speedup in scaling studies. Always launch with
    `mpirun -x OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 -x OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`. Worth adding to
    `BUILDING.md`.
13. **(00b-FBNS)** Timestep independence is **achieved at dt = 2e-5 s** on G6 with
    FBNS: mean eccentricity moves +0.0004% and +0.0002% over the next two halvings.
    dt = 4e-5 is not acceptable and dt = 8e-5 diverges.
14. No run hit `max_outer_iters` and no run stopped on non-positive film.
